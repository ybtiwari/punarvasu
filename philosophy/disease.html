How Disease Progresses

Disease follows a direction — from less vital organs toward organs vital for the sustenance of life. This is a rule.

No disease is without a cause.

Every illness has an origin — a constitutional susceptibility, a suppressed condition, or stress accumulated over time. We begin by understanding how the person arrived at this state.

Disease travels inward — as a rule.

As a disease progresses, it travels from the peripheral and less vital parts of the organism toward the organs more central to life. Emotions and skin conditions appear first. Then joints and digestion. Then the liver and kidneys. Then the heart and lungs. Then the brain and central nervous system. This is the natural path of every disease, without exception.

A person with long-standing stress develops acidity. Acidity, suppressed, becomes a gastric ulcer. Suppressed further, it affects the liver. The same thread — wound deeper at each stage.

Symptoms are the mirror of disease.

The symptoms a patient presents are the body's immune response — its way of communicating that something is disturbed. The more overwhelming the symptoms, the more serious the disease behind them. Suppressing a symptom removes the body's expression of the disease — not the disease itself.

Symptoms must be preserved — not suppressed. They are the measuring parameter of both the disease process and the curative process.
