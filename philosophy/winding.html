The Winding Metaphor

Why "Unwind" is the most accurate description of what this practice does.

Winding is what happens to a normal body.

A normal, healthy body is free of disease. Disease is not its original state — it is something that happens to it. Through stress, disturbance, suppression of symptoms, and constitutional susceptibility, the organism is pulled further from its natural state. Each layer of disturbance winds it a little tighter — inward, deeper, from the surface to the vital core.

Unwinding is the reverse.

Unwinding is the process of releasing each layer of disease in the reverse order it was acquired — from the inside outward, from the most vital to the least vital — until the organism returns to the state it was in before the winding began.

The disease did not arrive in a single moment. It accumulated. The cure must follow the same logic — accumulated layers, released one by one.

Why this metaphor is more suitable.

When we say winding, we mean a single piece of a thing being twisted. As it is twisted, the effect travels deeper within — but throughout, it remains one piece. The layers are not separate. This is precisely how the body works. A dysfunction on the surface and a dysfunction at the vital core are not two separate problems. They are two expressions of the same wound, at different depths.

This is why we do not treat organs separately. There is one winding to undo.
